# Optional voxel diffuse transport

Gevo uses this optional voxel adapter with an explicitly configured room volume. Shared SSGI remains separately available as described in `shared-ssgi.md`. Registering either transport explicitly clears the other, so their contributions never run concurrently.

The Windows player can compile the independent C++17 `helengine.gi.native` module. The dependency is disabled by default. Set `HELENGINE_ENABLE_VOXEL_GI=ON` and provide `HELENGINE_VOXEL_GI_ROOT` pointing at that module's source directory. The managed Windows builder derives these definitions from the process environment variable `HELENGINE_VOXEL_GI_ROOT`; an absent variable explicitly disables the feature, including in a previously enabled CMake cache.

The current rendering consumer is the Windows camera toon geometry pass, including its pearl material path. The common ForwardStandard shader and other platform adapters are not connected. Compiling the module does not silently add GI to those rendering paths.

At runtime, call `Win32RenderManager3D::ConfigureSceneGi` with the owning `Core`, one authored directional light, explicit finite volume and quality, and a positive work budget. Identical settings preserve the solver and published field. Changed volume settings initialize replacement resources before replacing the previous volume. Clear the configuration before destroying its borrowed scene/light or reusing the same settings with another `Core`.

The renderer scans `ObjectManager.Drawables3D`, independently of cameras, camera layers and output windows. Upload-time CPU copies supply mesh positions and indices; transforms and resolved per-submesh BaseColorBuffer values determine contributor revisions. Disabled or removed drawables are removed from transport. Zero-area triangles have zero contribution. Triangle and input validation failures remain visible exceptions.

This first adapter transports a single diffuse sun bounce from opaque triangle geometry. It uses the authored constant base color as albedo; diffuse texture detail, alpha-cutout silhouettes, translucent transport and emissive bounce are not represented. Surfaces with base-color alpha below 0.999 are excluded. Sun transport is independent of the renderer's direct-shadow toggle.

The finite field is shared by every camera and window and covers only its configured bounds. The solver stages work across updates, preserves the last completed generation during motion, and uploads the RGBA32F atlas only on publication. The per-update budget covers solver work; the authoritative metadata scan and changed-triangle conversion are measured separately and are not covered by that budget. Initial publications and every sixtieth publication report contributor count, traced rays, voxel tests, scan time and solver/upload time through native diagnostics.

The consumer reserves pixel-shader b9 and t17 during toon geometry draws and clears those bindings afterward. Alpha distinguishes empty probes, uniformly coplanar occupied cells and mixed occupied cells. Uniform occupied cells retain their canonical plane in otherwise unused irradiance channels. Trilinear sampling rejects occupied probes and blocked segments, reproducing the CPU's conservative boundary tie ordering. A receiver inside its own conservative plane footprint advances along its normal to the first empty cell within a bounded distance. A different plane or mixed cell blocks that traversal, including a separate roof sharing the receiver's voxel. This avoids tunneling through a second surface while correcting self-occlusion from conservative voxelization.

The optional `helengine_windows_scene_gi_tests` target runs owned offscreen WARP fixtures. It verifies HDR values, lobe weights, disabled/outside behavior, wall and window visibility, diagonal boundary ties, conservative surface exit with isotropic and anisotropic cells, CPU/GPU published-field agreement, and compilation of every production toon and pearl variant. It opens no window and captures no desktop content.


## Published-probe sphere diagnostic

After `ConfigureSceneGi`, call `ConfigureSceneGiDebugCameras(mainCamera, glassCamera)` with the existing main and shell cameras. Set `HELENGINE_GI_DEBUG_SPHERES=1` before starting the player to enable the diagnostic. Unset the variable, or set it to `0`, to use ordinary scene rendering with voxel GI. Other values, including an empty value, throw a visible configuration error. `ClearSceneGi` releases the diagnostic resources and borrowed camera identities together with the volume. Equal volume settings and repeated camera registration preserve the completed atlas and pipeline resources.

In diagnostic mode the main output clears to opaque dark gray (0.08 per channel) and draws the last completely published field. Before its first publication the field is intentionally empty. Each free cell accepted with positive interpolation weight by a visible surface fragment of either original camera produces one instanced sphere at its actual world-space center, with radius 0.18 times the smallest cell dimension. The bounded sphere mesh uses 192 procedural vertices and a single instanced draw, with ordinary depth testing and writes. Occupied and mixed cells are clipped using the atlas metadata. Unused probes are omitted even when bright; used probes remain visible even when their irradiance is zero. No new entities or GI contributors are created.

The pixel shader evaluates the existing six irradiance lobes using the sphere normal's signed axes and normalized absolute-axis weights. It converts that result to Rec.709 luminance and writes equal RGB values directly, without material colors, direct lighting, texture sampling, exposure, bloom, outlines, tonemapping or gamma correction. Raw values remain HDR in a floating-point target; a normalized presentation target naturally clips intensity above one. This visualization is a dense published voxel field, not a sparse octree or a visualization of direct sunlight.

The main and glass diagnostic cameras skip all ordinary 3D draws and camera post effects. Glass retains its existing clear/transparency behavior and ordinary 2D shell controls. Both cameras retain their authored poses, projections, clipping planes and world transforms. The sphere pass restores every pipeline binding it changes before ordinary 2D drawing. Normal-mode camera behavior is unchanged.

Native diagnostics emit `scene-gi-debug-spheres` with `mode=1 state=awaiting_published_field` on activation, followed by `state=published`, the generation and free/nonzero probe counts on each completed publication. Mode zero reports ordinary rendering. Existing `scene-gi-publication` entries retain transport timing and field statistics.

The independent `helengine_windows_gi_spheres_tests` target runs the actual production shader and pass on owned offscreen WARP textures, even when the optional transport module is disabled. Its fixtures check known raw intensities, directional grayscale, occupied-cell omission, depth ordering, disabled output and state restoration. These tests open no window and produce no desktop capture.


## Indirect-only scene surfaces

Set `HELENGINE_GI_INDIRECT_ONLY=1` before launch and retain the existing `ConfigureSceneGiDebugCameras` registration to show the real voxel contribution on the scene's original surfaces. This flag is mutually exclusive with `HELENGINE_GI_DEBUG_SPHERES=1`; unset it or use `0` for ordinary rendering. Malformed values throw. No core scene, light, camera, layer, geometry or contributor is added by this diagnostic.

The material shader preserves its existing alpha cutout and premultiplication but writes raw **RGB** `SampleSceneGi(worldPosition, normal) / pi`, equivalent to a neutral white diffuse receiver. Source albedo remains part of the portable transport, so colored bounce remains visible. Receiver material RGB, ambient, direct sun, emission, reflections and pearl highlights do not enter the displayed result. Zero indirect irradiance remains black. The actual authored sun still illuminates source surfaces inside the solver; increasing its incident intensity increases physically computed bounce rather than adding a display gain.

The existing geometry queues, depth, 1x/4x coverage, camera transforms and 2D shell controls remain active. Composition copies the resolved raw color without outlines, contact occlusion, bloom, exposure, tone mapping or gamma correction; bloom and contour prepasses are skipped. Clear RGB is black, preserving each output's normal clear alpha. Linear values above one clip naturally on a normalized presentation target. The mode uses reserved `GiCellSize.w` padding in the existing b9 field buffer and releases its bindings before UI. `scene-gi-indirect-only` startup diagnostics report the selected mode.

`helengine_windows_gi_indirect_only_tests` executes the actual production geometry and composite shaders on owned WARP textures. It verifies colored irradiance/pi independently of receiver color, direct illumination, pearl and emission; zero-input black; alpha/cutouts; unchanged raw HDR color under adversarial post-effect settings for both sample ABIs; and ordinary behavior when disabled. It needs neither the portable module nor desktop access.


## Exact receiver usage in sphere mode

The sphere diagnostic tracks the actual production `SampleSceneGi` tap selection, rather than brightness, distance to a mesh or one probe per object. A query must leave its own conservative surface footprint successfully. A tap is used only when it is inside the volume, free, visible from the validated query origin and has strictly positive interpolation weight. Accepted cells are committed only after the query's surviving total weight exceeds the sampler's existing `1e-8` normalization threshold. Irradiance RGB never affects usage: a used black probe remains a sphere.

Each original camera renders its ordinary 3D queue into an invisible, full-resolution, camera-local depth prepass using the same position/normal/UV transforms, diffuse-texture/base-color alpha cutoff (`0.001`), two-sided rasterization, D24 depth format, authored projection and 1x/4x coverage. A second pass uses early depth testing, equality and disabled depth writes, preventing hidden receiver fragments from marking probes. Its shader calls the shared production sampler with diagnostic-only atomic recording. Both camera passes OR into one GPU mask after a single clear. The sphere vertex shader reads that mask only after the reader UAV is unbound.

The field's scene update clock and published generation, both camera matrices/viewports/layer masks/enablement and sample count govern reuse between sequential output draws. Every new scene update or changed field/camera state clears and rebuilds the complete union, so removed or moved readers cannot leave stale probes. Grid replacement or diagnostic teardown releases the mask and depth resources. Before the first field publication the cleared mask stays empty. There is no normal-mode mask allocation, receiver pass or sampler instrumentation.

D3D11.1 context-state swapping isolates all diagnostic GPU bindings, including material resources, UAVs and outputs. Normal camera matrices and positions are restored on success and failure. Unsupported context-state creation and shader/resource failures propagate visibly. No synchronous GPU readback is used for live telemetry; `scene-gi-debug-spheres` reports `filter=receiver_weight cameras=2 stage=capture_ready` with the captured field generation. Existing `free_probes` counters describe the published field, not the number of drawn spheres.

The `helengine_windows_gi_usage_tests` target runs production reader shaders on owned WARP resources and covers used black probes, exact zero-weight exclusion, hidden receivers, camera union, stale removal after movement, alpha cutouts, 1x/4x coverage and context restoration. The sphere tests separately verify that the GPU mask suppresses unused lit probes while retaining used black probes. Tests produce no window, screenshot or desktop capture.
